5 · The system

What the verifier actually is

  • A LoRA adapter on the same 7B — 47.6 million parameters, 182 MB on disk
  • Trained on questions and images the evaluation never sees
  • Scores P(correct | image, question, candidate) and we keep the best
A LoRA adapter is not a model. It cannot see an image or read text on its own. It is a set of correction matrices applied on top of the 7B — so scoring one candidate costs a full pass through all 8.29 billion parameters.
6 · The system

We made the measurement stricter three times

  • Matched the prompts. Our two arms differed in the answer format they requested, as well as in the thing we were testing. Correcting it strengthened one finding and dissolved another.
  • Dropped a contaminated benchmark. The 7B scored 0.80 against its own published 54.0. Removing the image dropped it to 0.59; a control model scored 0.57.
  • Switched to equal weighting per cell.
Each one made our headline smaller — and we found all three ourselves, before anyone else read the work.

9 · The limits

The image encoder re-runs 294 tokens to recover five

  • Language prefill is shared across the eight candidates — 1.16×
  • The image encoder is not — 4.74× for eight answers
  • The image occupies positions 31–325; the cache is block-aligned to 320
Five tokens past a block boundary, so the engine cannot mark the image as already computed — and re-encodes all 294 of its tokens to recover the last five.

12 · The reframe

We were asking the wrong question

Can a small model plus extra computation match the big one?
How much can we improve the small model, for free?
  • On free text the big model is about ten times more efficient per point of accuracy
  • Sampling eight answers and scoring them costs more than asking the big model once — and is less accurate

16 · Closing

What did not work

  • Making the candidate pool more diverse. More variety, no better selection.
  • Re-asking the multiple-choice questions as free text. The model answers yes/no whether or not you offer it the options.
  • Deciding adaptively how many answers to sample. A fixed number, chosen once, beats it.
  • Voting among the sampled answers. Helps on two cells, does nothing on five, and hurts if you sample too few.
17 · Closing

Where we stand

  • Solid. A free, significant improvement that survives the hardest test we have — plus a much better understanding of what each component actually costs.
  • Open. The effect currently lives on one cell.
  • Next. Audit the remaining harness prompts for the same defect, and re-run the system end to end with the cost fixes in place.
If the same instruction defect exists in the other benchmarks, this stops being one result and becomes a general finding about how these systems are evaluated.
