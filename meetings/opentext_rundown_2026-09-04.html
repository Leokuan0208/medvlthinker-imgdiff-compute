00What the method is

Stated in the field’s vocabulary, since two of our habitual terms were internal coinages.

A lightweight MLP probe reads the frozen hidden states of an unmodified Lingshu-7B and acts as a best-of-N verifier: the 7B samples 8 candidate answers, the probe scores each, the top-scoring one is returned. It is measured against greedy decoding, an answer-prior baseline (a counter over answer strings — no image, no hidden state), and self-consistency.

Terminology

Per TERMINOLOGY_2026-08-24.md: benchmark not “cell”, probe + verifier not “MLP head”, candidate set not “pool”, answer-prior baseline not “string prior”. A head in the literature is trained jointly with a backbone; ours is a standalone module over a frozen model, which is a probe. Where both appear: probe verifier vs LoRA verifier.

Why GEMeX matters

It is the least memorisable benchmark in the project: 6,236 distinct questions, 4,316 distinct golds, top-10 gold coverage 12.7% — against RadImageNet 66.5%, SLAKE 41.9%, PathVQA 41.3%. A model cannot succeed there by learning the answer vocabulary.

3.5  The shipped artifact

ckpts/train/genframe_head_pooled_ens/ — 24 heads (3 layers × 8 seeds), 108,126 rows, BCE, per-layer frozen standardizers. Reload-verified at +0.0816 from disk against the incumbent recipe’s +0.0243 on the same halves.

Two constraints that travel with it

The incumbent genframe_head_ens8 is byte-intact and the freeze script hard-refuses to target it — it remains the artifact of record for every number published before 24 Aug.

The new artifact must not be evaluated on full benchmarks: it has seen the other image half of each, so the held-out halves are its only clean test set. That is written into its README, not just recorded here.

05Structural findings

The parts that explain the numbers rather than adding to them.

5.1  The decomposition — failures are the candidate set, not the ranker

verifier − greedy = selection skill − sampling penalty. Selection skill is positive on 7 of 8 benchmarks (mean +0.0403 against a mean penalty of +0.0256). VQA-RAD has +0.0244 of genuine skill and still loses, because its penalty is 0.0494. The probe is not failing to rank; it is ranking a candidate set that is worse than the model’s own greedy answer.

5.2  LOBO — the verifier is per-benchmark, not general

The benchmark’s own training half is worth +0.0500. The verifier learned eight per-benchmark rules, not something domain-general — so the shipped +0.0802 holds only where it has in-domain data. This is a materially weaker claim than the pooled number alone suggests, and it reconciles with the budget-matched breadth result: both say breadth is not the mechanism.

5.3  The price of onboarding a benchmark — ~100 labelled questions

From the deployable base. 6/8 already beat greedy at k=0, and the first hundred questions carry most of the gain: OmniMedVQA −0.0004 → +0.0986, RadImageNet +0.0010 → +0.0528, GEMeX +0.0224 → +0.0857. Then it flattens.

Unlike the layer effect, pooling did not flatten this — the spread is 0.0416. This retires the earlier per-benchmark cold-temperature finding as an artifact of the weaker four-domain probe. Mechanistically it fits: a stronger verifier prefers more diverse candidate sets because it can exploit the extra coverage.

5.5  GEMeX — the cleanest evidence of real verification

Verifier +0.0148 [+0.0057, +0.0238] over greedy, and +0.0573 [+0.0483, +0.0663] over the answer prior — where that prior itself loses to greedy by 0.0425. It is also the most out-of-distribution benchmark we have (largest kNN distance 7.228, lowest domain-classifier confidence 0.693), and the verifier wins on it anyway. So distance from the training distribution does not predict where the method helps.

06Corrections to our own claims

Eleven claims that did not survive. Each is recorded in the artifact of record rather than quietly replaced.

  1. A stale checkpoint invalidated every OmniMedVQA number. run_openvqa.py resumed on idx alone; the rebuild re-indexed 0..8882 over a different question set, so regeneration reported success in 160 s having generated nothing. Only 17 of 5,000 questions matched. Corrected: −0.0489 → −0.0193, and the answer prior does not beat the verifier there. Caught because a fresh T=0.4 run produced an impossible number. Fixed at source with a question-text guard.
  2. The router’s “WIN” was my confidence interval. +0.0133 [+0.0109, +0.0156] resampling questions i.i.d. → +0.0119 [−0.0032, +0.0297] clustered by benchmark, where essentially all the variance lives. A tie.
  3. Mixed currency in the decomposition. Exact-match labels for one term, judge labels for the others — inflated the sampling penalty roughly eightfold. Caught before it reached a deck.
  4. Breadth confounded with volume. The k-domain curve trained k=6 on six times the rows of k=1, so it could not separate “more domains” from “more data” — the question it existed to answer.
  5. Kvasir counted twice. The same GI-endoscopy source appeared as two “independent” domains, carrying both the two largest slopes and the two largest spreads. Collapsed, the breadth interval includes zero.
  6. The sampling conclusion drawn from the wrong two benchmarks. “Doubling the budget buys nothing” came from VQA-RAD and VQA-Med — the two lowest-gain of the eight, chosen because they were coverage-limited, which is exactly the wrong sample. Macro is +0.0164.
  7. The self-consistency sign-flip “with no exceptions” has an exception: GEMeX is out-of-training and the verifier beats SC by +0.0328.
  8. “The regime is detectable” did not survive one additional benchmark — precisely the fragility flagged when it was first reported at n=7.
  9. MedPix overlap, 19 images shared between vqa_rad_open_train and VQA-Med. Immaterial (those questions score 0.0000 for both arms, so removing them moves −0.0259 to −0.0261) but removed anyway.
  10. The AdamW landmine. foreach=False does not fix the segfault — it moves it. The real variable is thread count: 10 threads dies in ~30 s, 4 runs clean.
  11. A stub counted as success. A numpy int64 made json.dump raise after each layer’s fit, so every layer was computed and discarded, leaving a 126-byte file the planner read as “done”.

07Infrastructure

Six failures that cost real time, and what each one taught.

  • A failed producer deadlocks its consumers. A chained campaign waited 21 hours on an artifact whose producing job had already failed. A chain must wait on “produced or failed”.
  • Fixed queues cannot keep GPUs busy. The runnable set grows as jobs land — each new judge file makes an extraction runnable. Replaced with a planner that reads the tree each wave and emits only jobs whose inputs exist and outputs do not.
  • The driver exited on an empty queue. “Nothing runnable” usually means “nothing runnable yet”. Cost four idle GPU-days. Now sleeps and re-plans.
  • A self-refilling planner is only as deep as its catalogue. It later idled 33 hours with the driver alive and re-planning correctly, because every job type it knew had finished.
  • Two jobs, one card. vLLM reserves a fixed fraction of the GPU at startup, so two jobs do not share — a leftover process holding 22.8 GB killed a run twice. Every GPU job now runs behind a per-card lock.
  • GEMeX provenance. The licence warning printed unconditionally, so a --source physionet run reported third-party re-hosting that was untrue of it — and would have sent us re-pulling 21,312 images we already had.

09What I would claim today

Positive

A frozen 918k-parameter MLP probe reading one layer of an unmodified 7B beats greedy decoding on 4 of 8 open-ended medical VQA benchmarks and beats an answer-frequency baseline on all 8 — including GEMeX, where that baseline loses to greedy outright. Retrained on all eight and rank-ensembled over three layers it reaches +0.0802 macro against +0.0243 for the original recipe.

Mechanism

verifier − greedy = selection skill − sampling penalty. Skill is positive on 7 of 8 benchmarks, so where the method fails it fails because the sampled candidate set is worse than greedy decoding — not because the ranker is bad.

Deployment

The method needs a labelled split per benchmark — breadth alone buys nothing — and that split costs ~100 labelled questions.

The honest hole

We cannot predict which benchmarks the method helps. Distance from the training distribution does not do it (GEMeX is the furthest and the verifier wins there), answer kind does not, and no detector orders the benchmarks. Twelve documented dead ends bound the alternatives, several with measured ceilings rather than mere nulls — which is the contribution that survives regardless of how the positive claim lands.
