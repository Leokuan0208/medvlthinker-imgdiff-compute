The method, in full

What it is, what every component contributes, what it scores, what it costs, and which of the three configurations you should actually quote. All figures are macro — equal weight per reporting cell, 8 cells, MMMU excluded — measured with the decontaminated verifier.

1 · What the method is

A format-aware adaptive cascade between two models of the same family. It reads the prompt, decides whether the question is multiple-choice or open-ended, and runs a different policy for each. Both policies end with the same escape hatch: hand the question to the big model.

Stage 0 · Router

Format detection from the prompt text alone

Decides multiple-choice vs open-ended by inspecting the prompt template — never the gold answer. This is reliable because the harness's own templates differ structurally: option-bearing prompts supply an answer space, open ones do not.

Stage 1a · Multiple-choice arm — 5 of 8 cells, 62.5% of the score

7B-direct → confidence-margin gate → 32B-direct

The 7B answers directly. The gate reads the margin — the gap between the top-1 and top-2 option log-probabilities — and escalates to the 32B when the margin falls below a cross-fit threshold. No reasoning trace is generated at any point.

A certified veto sits on top: where a Wilson lower bound on the 7B's precision in a confidence bin exceeds the 32B's accuracy in that same bin, the 7B's answer is kept and the 32B is never called. It answers — it is not abstention.

Stage 1b · Open-text arm — 3 of 8 cells, 37.5% of the score

7B best-of-N → trained verifier → escalate on low confidence

The 7B samples up to 8 candidate answers, with a Weitzman optimal-stopping rule deciding when to stop drawing (mean 5.5 of 8). A LoRA verifier scores P(correct | image, question, candidate) for each, and the argmax is taken. If the verifier's top score is low, the question escalates to the 32B instead.

The deployed selector is a rank fusion of that verifier with a frozen 8-seed discriminative head reading the generator's own hidden states.

On four of the five multiple-choice cells the method is byte-identical to always-32B-direct. The gate escalates, the veto never fires, and the output is the baseline's. On 50% of the macro weight the method is the thing it is being compared against.

The entire vs-direct delta is therefore two cells: PMC-VQA (+0.0095) and PathVQA-open (+0.0087), less what SLAKE-open and VQA-RAD-open give back. Drop PMC-VQA and the delta goes negative. And PMC-VQA is the split with zero published verification — an audit found 44% of its gain traceable to answer-position skew, and 53% of audited veto wins sitting on defective labels.

Where the compute actually goes

Decomposing the shipped arm's 7.951 FLOP-eq:

  • By leg — 32B escalation 46.4%, cheap-leg generation 27.6%, verifier scoring 26.0%, selector head 0.0%.
  • By phase — LM prefill 82.1%, vision towers 16.7%, all decode 1.2%. This is a prefill-bound workload. Every idea about generating fewer or shorter answers was doomed before it started; the only levers are fewer forward passes and fewer prompt/image tokens.
  • Pure waste — 44.1% of macro compute is cheap-side work on questions that get escalated anyway.
  • The three open cells are 3/8 of the weight but 65.4% of the cost.

The entire open-text arm — 8-sample generator and verifier co-resident — runs in 18.76 GiB on a single 24 GB card, because the verifier is an adapter on the generator's own base and adds 0.196 GiB. always-32B-direct needs an 80 GB card and nothing smaller. But any escalation puts both models in memory: 77.76 GiB of weights plus activations exceeds one 80 GB card, so the deployed method is a two-card system. Going 7B-only is not a gradual saving — it is a hardware-class cliff.

5 · The three configurations, and which to quote

Configuration A · the shipped method

accuracy-max — macro 0.6575 at 1.74× compute

What the spec and the paper describe. Wins against a reasoning 32B by +0.0601 [+0.0499, +0.0700] at −87.9% parallel latency and −84.3% energy; ties a direct 32B (+0.0008). Quote this if the claim is about reasoning models.

Configuration B · the cost-optimal point

macro 0.6569 at 0.865× compute, −2.2% latency, −8.2% energy

Certified veto on PMC-VQA, compute-lean on SLAKE-closed and PathVQA-closed, and always-32B-direct on VQA-RAD-closed, MedXpert and all three open cells. Tie with the baseline (+0.0002 [−0.0019, +0.0023]), guardrail-clean on all 8 cells, and cheaper on all three cost axes at once. The arm assignment was pre-specified from an earlier cross-fit, not chosen on this evaluation.

The uncomfortable part: it reaches that by switching the open-text machinery off. Exhaustive enumeration of all 1,679,616 per-cell assignments shows nothing below ~0.86× can satisfy the accuracy constraint at all.

Configuration C · the MCQ-only restriction

+0.0012 [+0.0009, +0.0015] at 0.977× — the only CI-clean win

Shipped MCQ policy, open half left at always-32B-direct. Formally beats the bar, but it is 100% one cell — byte-identical to the baseline on seven of eight by construction, so drop PMC-VQA and the delta is exactly zero. Post-hoc and exploratory. Do not lead with it.

My recommendation. Lead with the reasoning comparison from Configuration A and the cost result from Configuration B: "matches a 32B forward pass at 0.87× its compute, −2.2% latency and −8.2% energy, and beats a reasoning 32B by +0.060 at a tenth of its latency and energy." Both halves are measured, guardrail-clean, and survive every correction applied so far.

6 · The limits, measured

Free upper bounds on the 8-cell macro. These are what a perfect version of each component would buy.

  • Perfect selection over the current 8-sample pools: +0.0301.
  • Perfect coverage (infinite sampling): +0.0091. Selection headroom is 3.3× coverage headroom.
  • Perfect 7B-vs-32B routing: +0.0661 — of which 1.3% is currently converted. The ceiling is not the problem; identifiability is.
  • Selection efficiency is stuck at 0.78–0.81 across ~27 distinct architectures. This is a field constant, externally confirmed — an independent 2026 system converts 78.3% of its oracle; we convert 77.5%.
  • Seed spread (~0.021) exceeds every architectural effect we have measured.
  • A no-32B pipeline would need open-text selection efficiency of 0.988 to reach parity. It cannot get there.

7 · Caveats that constrain every number above

  1. ±0.008 open-text reproducibility. Regenerating the 32B open arm under a different tensor-parallel configuration moves cells by ±0.008 — larger than the entire vs-direct delta. Every open-text comparison needs a matched control in the same serving configuration.
  2. Nothing was run end to end. Every operating point is a CPU re-costing of saved per-sample dumps with per-leg batch-1 constants. The one genuine execution in the project is a June-era cascade.
  3. PMC-VQA is load-bearing and unverified. It carries the vs-direct claim, uses the split with no published verification, has B+C = 73.6% answer-position skew, and 53% of audited veto wins sit on defective labels.
  4. A grading defect in the harness itself. A bare "C:" response is reduced to the empty string and falls through to fuzzy text matching — and it differentially penalises the 32B. One apparent win this week was entirely this artifact.
  5. MMMU-Medical is excluded on contamination grounds (the 7B scores 0.80 against its own published 54.0). Under macro it would carry 11.1%, so the exclusion is consequential and must be argued on those grounds alone.
  6. vLLM silently drops all 192 visual LoRA modules — the same adapter scores 0.775 under HuggingFace and 0.703 under vLLM. Never score a visual adapter under vLLM.
  7. Four numerics landmines, each larger than most real effects here: TF32 defaults (up to 0.024), CPU thread count, feature row order, ranker choice.
