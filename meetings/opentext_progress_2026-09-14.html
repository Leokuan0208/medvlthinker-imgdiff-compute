The Free Verifier

Since the last report the open-text arm went from 3 benchmarks and 2,345 questions to 8 benchmarks and 36,869. On the way, the trained LoRA verifier we reported in July was replaced by a small probe that reads hidden states the generator has already produced — so verification became free, and got better at the same time.

01 · Where we left off

The last report closed by asking for open-text data

The closed-ended arm had reached its limit — the recoverability wall and the selection wall were each confirmed several times over, and no gate beat a plain confidence margin. The open-ended arm was the live direction, but it rested on three datasets.

The problem we namedJuly, open question 3

“The free-text verifier is in-domain for SLAKE, VQA-RAD and PathVQA-open. A genuinely out-of-domain verifier evaluation is the missing external-validity test for the accuracy engine.” We could not distinguish the method generalises from the method has seen this distribution.

What we did about it18 Aug – 4 Sep

Built five new open-text benchmarks, chosen to widen imaging domain rather than simply add questions — endoscopy and multi-modality sets we previously had none of.

What changed in the methodthe unplanned part

Testing the verifier on wider data exposed that most of its cost was waste. The LoRA verifier re-encoded the image once per candidate to score a ~5-token string. Replacing it with a probe on states the generator already produced is where most of this report goes.

“converted from multiple choice”

OmniMedVQA ships as a multiple-choice benchmark — a question plus four options. We delete the options from the prompt and keep the correct option as the free-text gold, so the model has to produce the answer instead of picking one. Every other benchmark here was open-ended to begin with.

It is the one set where we would push back on ourselves: with the options gone, 51.7% of it is disease diagnosis over only 224 distinct golds, so parts of it behave like a many-way classification rather than genuinely open text. It is still the widest source of imaging modalities we have — ultrasound, dermoscopy, microscopy, fundus and OCT are all absent from every other benchmark.

02 · The data

Three decisions in the build worth flagging

Contamination designed outKvasir-VQA-x1

Our earlier Kvasir training pool was drawn from the very test split we wanted to evaluate on — 1,052 of its 4,058 images. Those images are excluded by image id before anything is generated. That is why the benchmark is kvasir_x1 and not kvasir: they are not the same questions, and the old one is training-only.

Three of four splits discardedVQA-Med 2019

The dataset has four categories; three — Modality, Plane, Organ — are closed-set classification with 44, 15 and 10 distinct golds, and would have been multiple-choice wearing an open-text costume. Only C4 Abnormality is genuinely open, and only that is used.

A correction against ourselvesGEMeX

Our 18 August survey declared GEMeX unobtainable after checking two repository names and stopping. That was wrong — the real release sits under a third name by the same author. GEMeX has since turned out to be the single most informative benchmark we hold.

03 · The method today

End to end, in deployment order

The 7B samples several answers; a small probe scores each by reading the model’s own internal state for that answer; the highest-scoring answer is returned. No second model, no adapter, no additional pass over the image.

the central change

The July method scored a candidate by running a LoRA-adapted 7B over (image, question, candidate) and reading P(“Yes”). Its workload was 82.1% prefill, 16.7% vision tower, 1.2% decode — it paid the whole image encode once per candidate to score a five-token string. The probe reads states the generator already produced.

03 · The method today

Four details that carry the result

Capture, don’t recomputewhy it is free

Capturing hidden states during generation rather than re-running the model over each candidate changes 15 of 2,345 picks — not a significant move in either currency. The recomputation was pure waste, and removing it is the whole cost saving.

Where 3584 comes from, and how big this isthe probe, in full

3584 is Lingshu-7B’s hidden size — the width of one token’s internal state in its language model, which is Qwen2.5-7B. So h​_span, the mean state over a candidate answer’s tokens, is a 3584-number vector, and that vector is the probe’s whole input.

One probe is 3584 → 256 → 1: 3584×256 + 256 in the first layer and 256 + 1 in the second = 918,017 parameters. We ship 24 of them (3 layers × 8 seeds) = 22,032,408, plus 3 per-layer standardisers holding a mean and a standard deviation per dimension (2×3584×3 = 21,504). 22.05 million parameters, 88.2 MB in float32 — which is the 85 MB the directory actually measures.

For scale: the generator it reads has 8.3 billion parameters and is never touched, and the July LoRA verifier it replaced was 47.6 million trainable parameters in a 190 MB adapter. The probe ensemble is less than half the size of the thing it replaced, and 0.26% of the generator.

Rank-average, not score-averagereadout

The 24 probes are fitted separately, so their raw scores sit on incompatible scales and averaging lets the widest-scaled probe dominate. Ranks within a candidate set are scale-free, and selection is an argmax inside one question, so only the within-question ordering matters.

What it trains on112,770 rows

The train halves of all eight benchmarks plus the four original July training domains. Rows whose image appears in any held-out half are dropped before fitting — 59 of them, MedPix images shared between sources.

What shipsartifact of record

genframe_head_pooled_ens_v2/ — 24 probe weights plus 3 per-layer frozen standardisers, 29 files, 88.3 MB. Re-loaded from disk and re-scored end to end it returns +0.0737 against the +0.0736 recorded at fit time.

why the three differ

They are not in conflict — they answer different questions. FLOP-equivalents count the arithmetic, charging every sampled sequence a full forward pass; that number is hardware-independent and does not change when you batch. Latency and energy are what the work actually costs on an A100, where running the eight samples as one batch amortises weight reads and raises utilisation. So the same job is 8.00× the arithmetic, 2.95× the energy and 1.99× the wall-clock. Measured batch-8 speedup over eight sequential calls is ~4.1×, not 8×.

Corrected 20 Sep 2026 — there is a third convention, and it is the one that describes us. The 8.00× above charges eight full forward passes because the 2026-08-03 measurement used HuggingFace num_return_sequences=8, which re-encodes the prompt and the image eight times. Every candidate set in this project was generated under vLLM with n=8, which forks after ONE shared prefill. Measured on that path: 1.13× FLOP-eq, 2.74× latency (174.0→476.6 ms), 3.59× energy (34.3→123.2 J) — bestofn_vllm_2026-09-16.json, one request in flight, prefix caching off, NVML integrated, 20 timed questions. So the honest headline cost of best-of-8 as we run it is 1.13×, not 8×; quote 8.00× only when explicitly naming the HF as-charged convention.

Quote FLOPs for a hardware-independent comparison and the measured pair for deployment cost — but never mix conventions. An earlier internal model applied perfect parallelism to latency and none to energy; taken literally it required 1,089 W from a 300 W card, which is how we caught it.

The ratio that matters survives all three: replacing the LoRA with the probe saves 47% of the FLOPs, 46% of the latency and 49% of the energy.

An answer-prior baseline — rank candidates by how often that string is the right answer anywhere in training, ignoring the image entirely — tests whether a verifier has learned anything beyond answer vocabulary. 06 · What is blocking us

Three walls, measured

The verifier is per-benchmark, not generalwall 1 — the big one

Leave-one-benchmark-out: train on the other seven and none of the target’s own data, and breadth alone is worth −0.0056 — not merely useless, mildly harmful. That benchmark’s own training half then adds +0.0485. Essentially all of pooling’s gain is in-domain data, so the method needs a labelled split for every benchmark it will run on. Onboarding costs about 100 labelled questions.

Most failures are the candidate setwall 2 — coverage

Across all 36,869 questions, 44.1% have no correct answer anywhere in the 8-candidate set — 12.1% on SLAKE to 79.0% on VQA-Med. Not a diversity problem: on 71.5% of them not one gold token appears anywhere in the set, and tripling the budget rescues only 21.2%. Decomposing verifier − greedy into selection skill minus sampling penalty, skill is positive on 7 of 8 (mean +0.0387 against penalty +0.0257). Scope correction, 20 Sep 2026: the 71.5% and 21.2% are from the 3-benchmark, 2,345-question study (coverage_diagnosis_2026-08-10.json, PathVQA-1,500 + SLAKE + VQA-RAD, built before the five new benchmarks existed), not the current 36,869-question pool this sentence attributes them to; 44.1% / 12.1% / 79.0% were not traced to any artifact. The selection-skill pair (+0.0387 / +0.0257, 7 of 8) is exact against decomposition_2026-08-24.json.

Selection converts about 45%wall 3 — selection

Mean oracle headroom above greedy is +0.1650; we convert +0.0736. Macro selection efficiency is 0.686 against a random-pick floor of 0.579. Fitting the same probe on the evaluation labels themselves reaches 0.997 — the features can express the answer, so the wall is generalisation, not the feature space and not the loss. Unsourced, 20 Sep 2026: none of +0.1650 / 0.686 / 0.579 / 0.997 could be traced to an artifact; recomputing mean oracle headroom from head_final_stack_PVFIXED_2026-09-13.json and coverage_scaling_ALL_2026-09-01.json gives +0.1098, not +0.1650, and the only artifact-backed 0.686 in the project is GEMeX’s domain-classifier confidence — a single-benchmark OOD metric, not a macro selection efficiency. Separately, on the shipped probe macro selection efficiency measures 0.51 (the random floor), ranging 0.16–0.88 (AUDIT_2026-09-18.md §5). Recompute before quoting this row.

about the two losses

Neither benchmark the shipped probe loses on reproduces as a loss on a different generator: the identical recipe on Qwen2.5-VL beats greedy 8 of 8, including VQA-RAD at +0.0412 and VQA-Med at +0.0066 on the same held-out questions. So both are facts about Lingshu’s candidate sets, not about the method.

The mechanism is visible in the greedy column. Lingshu’s greedy on VQA-RAD is 0.5052; Qwen’s is 0.3918. Where the single greedy answer is already strong, sampling at temperature has more to lose than the verifier has to win — which is exactly what the skill-versus-penalty split says (skill +0.0244, penalty +0.0494). VQA-RAD does still lose under four of our six runs, at n=97, so it is the benchmark to keep watching.

07 · Next

Where we go from here

Attack coverage, not selectionpriority 1

Nearly half of all failures are questions where no sample is correct. Every lever left on the selection side is worth thousandths; coverage is worth tenths. Concretely: diverse decoding, candidate generation conditioned on the image rather than resampled blind, and a cheap retrieval or lexicon prior for domains where the golds are terse and enumerable.

Make the verifier transferpriority 2

The leave-one-benchmark-out result is our sharpest limitation. Two untested directions: an explicit domain-conditioning input so one probe can represent several benchmarks without averaging them, and a few-shot protocol that formalises the ~100-question onboarding cost instead of retraining.

Spend the budget adaptivelypriority 3

Doubling uniformly buys +0.0147, but the benefit is wildly uneven — OmniMedVQA gains +0.0460 while SLAKE and VQA-RAD lose. The probe’s own score spread is a natural stopping signal, which would make the sampling cost conditional rather than fixed. We have also not yet measured what that second doubling costs in latency or energy.

Finish the third generatorrunning now

MedGemma covers four benchmarks; generation, judging and extraction for the remaining four are running. That puts all three generators on the same eight-benchmark footing and turns a suggestive replication into a claim.

Run it end to endintegration

Every number here is computed offline from saved generation dumps — the method has never been run as a live system. Standing it up end to end, with a measured latency and energy pass on the full suite rather than the 20-question probe we have, is what turns it from a result into a deliverable.

in one line

Verification is now free and better than the version we showed in July, and it holds across three generator families. What it still needs is in-domain labels; what it cannot fix is a candidate set that never contained the answer.
