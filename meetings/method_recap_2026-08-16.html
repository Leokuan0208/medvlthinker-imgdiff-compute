What a deployer needs. The cheap leg alone wants a 24 GB card (23.42 GiB peak is 97.6% full — take 32 GB for headroom). The entire open-text arm — 8-sample generator and verifier co-resident — fits in 18.76 GiB on a single 24 GB card, because the verifier is a LoRA adapter on the generator's own base and adds just 0.196 GiB: the arm costs one 7B, not two. always-32B-direct needs an 80 GB card and nothing smaller. A 3.9× smaller footprint at the accuracy tie.

Three numbers, three different things. The circulating vLLM figures (71.45 / 152.82 GB) are memory-pool reservations — a property of the --gpu_mem flag, which is why three different 7B models report an identical 71.45. vLLM's own "model weights take X GiB" line is honest but weights-only and per tensor-parallel worker (31.28 × 2 = 62.56 for the 32B). The HF measurement lands at 15.49 / 62.31 — within 0.5%, cross-validating both, and independently confirmed by a tp=1 probe at 62.43.

Two things the measurement overturned. Reasoning does not cost more memory: 3 → 259 generated tokens moves the footprint by +0.09 GiB, because the peak is set by prefill of a long multimodal prompt, not by decode — so the circulating "think adds 1.26 GB" was never a KV-cache measurement, just two different pool reservations. And the token audit caught an error in the measurement itself: pairing the reasoning prompt with MedEvalKit's MCQ tail produced 2.6 generated tokens — the exact pseudo-reasoning trap this project documents — because "directly" sits closer to the generation point and overrides the system prompt. That run is retained in the artifact marked do not quote.

Compute against accuracy

Each point is a measured operating point. The dashed line is always-32B-direct; anything below it loses on accuracy, anything right of 1.0× costs more than simply running the big model once. The cluster hugging the line from both sides is the whole story of this project.

statistical tie with the bar significant loss baseline oracle (upper bound)

Read the horizontal spread, not the vertical. Every clean operating point sits within 0.003 accuracy of the bar — inside the confidence interval — while spanning 1.0× to 4.3× its compute. Eight pre-registered attacks across two rounds have not moved a point above the dashed line with a CI excluding zero.

The one exception is retired: the two dashed circles above the line used a verifier trained on ~70% of the items it scored. Decontamination costs −0.0119 [−0.0188, −0.0052] and turns that win into a tie.

What the numbers are not

Eight conventions that decide whether two rows in the table above can be compared at all. Most of this project's historical errors were comparisons across these lines.

  1. Weighting. Macro gives each of 8 reporting cells 1/8. Sample-weighted gives PMC-VQA 79.2% of the suite. The same method reads 1.196× macro and 0.492× sample-weighted — never pair a macro accuracy with a sample-weighted cost.
  2. Three harnesses. (A) faithful MedEvalKit, PMC-VQA = test_2.csv, 33,430 items, zero published verification. (B) internal NGC, PMC-VQA = test_clean.csv, 2,000 items, human-verified. (C) a custom judged open-text pipeline. The headline pool is a splice of A and C. test_clean ∩ test_2 = 6 items.
  3. Contamination. Every open-text cell of the published headline used a verifier trained on ~70% of the items it scored. Decontaminating costs −0.0119 [−0.0188, −0.0052] and turns "beats always-32B-direct" into a tie.
  4. Cost convention. As-charged uses R32 = 4.57; the honestly derived R32 = 3.816 moves every ratio against the method. The assumed vLLM prefill-sharing credit is void — vLLM V1 splits n=N into N child requests each carrying the full prompt.
  5. Engine. vLLM 0.9.0.1 silently drops all 192 visual.* LoRA modules: the same adapter scores 0.775204 under HF and 0.702997 under vLLM. Its decode is also non-deterministic across tensor-parallel settings — open cells reproduce only to ±0.008 per cell, which is larger than the entire published headline delta.
  6. The reasoning baseline is two different things. Prompt-unmatched it scores 0.5974 but is not a reasoning run on ~90% of the pool (3.0–3.3 generated tokens). Prompt-matched it scores 0.6250, and our win against it halves.
  7. Nothing was run end to end. Every Lingshu-era operating point is a CPU re-costing of saved per-sample dumps using per-leg batch-1 constants. The single genuine execution is the June live cascade.
  8. Two paper constants are refuted. Best-of-N latency and energy were asserted at 522 ms / 568.8 J; measured they are 1,305.3 ms / 316.7 J (n=45). "N drops out of latency" is false.

The VRAM gap

You asked for VRAM. It is the one column the project cannot honestly fill, and the reason is worth stating precisely.

160 of 229 rows have no VRAM figure, and the submitted paper contains none at all. The nine numbers that circulate — 71.45, 152.82, 154.08 GB and so on — are vLLM memory-pool reservations set by a --gpu_mem flag, allocated per model tier. The tell: three different 7B models all report exactly 71.45 GB, and three different 32B models all report exactly 152.82 GB.

One genuine measurement exists — the June co-resident cascade, 18,014 MB (7B) + 68,321 MB (32B) = 86,335 MB — and it lives in a log file with no artifact, as a resident-after-load snapshot rather than a peak.

vLLM's own honest figures for weights alone (7B 15.63 GiB, 32B 31.46 GiB, 38B 37.87 GiB) are cited nowhere. The INT4 strong leg claimed "a real VRAM win" with no measured number behind it — the quantised shards never downloaded.

✓ Measured 2026-08-11 — see the table above. Five test-time scenarios on HuggingFace rather than vLLM. The per-method rows below still read not measured, and that remains honest: what exists now is a footprint per tier and pipeline stage, not per operating point.

Still uncharacterised: batch > 1, tp=2 footprint, 7B+32B co-residency on one card, and a resolution-cap ablation — that last is the direct VRAM lever, since the peak is prefill-bound and driven by vision-token count (46,816 on the worst item).
