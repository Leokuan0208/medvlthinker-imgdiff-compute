Medical visual question answering: two answer formats

SLAKE, abdominal CT (question 12231).
Closed-endedanswer from a fixed set: yes/no, or a letter among options (multiple choice). Grading is trivial: string equality. “Is the liver visible?” → yes
Open-ended (free text) — this workthe model writes the answer. Many correct strings exist (“large bowel”, “colon”, “large intestine”), so grading needs either exact match after normalisation or a grader model (LLM-as-a-judge). “What organ is the gray part on the left of the image?” → gold: Large Bowel
Why open-endedit is what a clinician-facing system faces, and it is where selection signals work: in this project, error detection reached AUROC ≈ 0.87 on free text vs ≈ 0.6 on 4-option multiple choice.

Decoding: greedy vs sampling, and what temperature does

greedy decodingalways take the most likely next token. One answer, deterministic. Our baseline.
temperature samplingdraw the next token at random from $$p_i=\frac{e^{u_i/T}}{\sum_j e^{u_j/T}}$$ \(u_i\) = the model's logit for token i. T → 0 is greedy; larger T spreads probability, so repeated samples differ. Ours: plain temperature sampling, T = 0.7, max 64 tokens; no top-p / top-k set (run_openvqa.py).
not to be confused withtemperature scaling, a calibration method that rescales a classifier's logits after training. Unrelated.
distinct answers among 8 samples (macro mean)
oracle@8 — at least one of the 8 is correct (judge, macro)
Both y-axes are truncated (they do not start at 0).

Published medical test-time selectors: WED and Best-of-Evidence

Hagen et al. 2026 · arXiv:2605.18313

Wasserstein Equilibrium Decoding (WED)

  1. sample ≤ 8 distinct candidates (≤ 16 draws), merge by meaning
  2. same VLM scores each as generator and as verifier
  3. the two roles update toward each other (a game)
  4. stop when Wasserstein-1 < 0.2; return the top candidate
training-free · VQA-RAD, PathVQA open · −1.2 to +5.0 pts over greedy · “gains diminish with size”
