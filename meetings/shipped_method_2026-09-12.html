End to end

What actually runs, per question

Why the ranking step is not a detail. The 24 probes are fitted separately and are not on a common scale — one outputs ±0.05, another ±8. Averaging raw scores lets the loudest probe decide. Each probe’s scores are converted to ranks within that question’s candidate set first, then the 24 rank vectors are averaged. Getting this wrong is worth 0.008, larger than several effects we chased.
The input

The probe reads one thing, and it is already there

h_span

The 7B processes [system][image][question][candidate]. At layer 20, every token position holds a 3584-number hidden state. h_span is the mean over the positions belonging to that candidate answer.

So two candidates for the same question have different vectors — which is what lets the probe rank them.

What it is not given

Not the image pixels. Not the question text. Not the answer string. Not sampling frequency. Not logprobs.

Each of those was tested. See the limits slide.

Why three layers

Single layers score within 0.013 of each other and three different metrics pick three different winners — CV says 20, transfer says 18, end-to-end says 19. That spread is noise, not signal.

Ensembling 18/20/22 beats every single layer and beats concatenating them.

Why 8 seeds each

A single fit is a lottery ticket: the 1-seed recipe spans [0.8025, 0.8140] across 16 seeds. That 0.012 spread is larger than most effects we measure.

Averaging 8 collapses it, so a difference is the change and not the draw.

Training data did the work

+0.0547 of the +0.0554 total. The four new benchmarks had contributed zero training rows, so every number on them was zero-shot transfer.

The ensemble is a hedge, not a boost

+0.0007. Worth taking because it is free and removes a knob — after pooling, the single-layer spread collapses from 0.013 to 0.003.

One component vanished

Self-consistency as an input was worth +0.0098 from the four-domain base and −0.0016 on macro once pooled — though it does flip VQA-Med positive, taking 6/8 to 7/8. It was compensating for missing data, not adding signal — so shipping it would buy an inference-time dependency for free.

Cost

What it costs to run

The probe itself

~0

24 MLPs of 918k parameters, ~1.8 MFLOP each, on a vector the generator already produced. No pass over the image, no second model loaded.

The sampling

8×

This is the real cost and it is the generator’s, not the probe’s. Best-of-N needs N samples.

Doubling to 16

+0.0147

Measured at matched budget on identical questions, 6 of 8 positive. A genuine Pareto point — but it doubles generation, unlike everything else here.

Onboarding a benchmark

~100 q

Labelled questions before a new benchmark beats greedy, measured from a probe trained on the other seven.

The honest framing of the cost. The probe is free; the 8 samples are not. Against a single greedy call this is 8× the generation for +0.08 macro. Against the alternative ways of spending that 8× — self-consistency, a bigger pool, a colder pool, more candidates — the probe is the only one that converted it, and we measured all four.
Limits · what this method does not do

Two benchmarks it loses on, and why

benchmarkgreedyprobeoracle@8diagnosis
VQA-Med C40.09130.08800.2102Coverage, not selection. Most candidate sets contain no correct answer at all — there is nothing for any selector to find.
VQA-RAD0.50520.47420.6300Too little data. Its training half is 365 rows; it is the smallest benchmark we have at 200 questions.
The decomposition says the ranker is not the problem. verifier − greedy = selection skill − sampling penalty. Selection skill is positive on 7 of 8 benchmarks (mean +0.0403 against a mean penalty of +0.0256). VQA-RAD has +0.0244 of genuine skill and still loses, because sampling at T=0.7 costs it 0.0494. Where the method fails, it fails because the sampled candidate set is worse than the model’s own greedy answer.
The limit that constrains deployment. Leave-one-benchmark-out: training on seven benchmarks and testing on the eighth is worth −0.0008. The benchmark’s own training half is worth +0.0500. The probe learned eight per-benchmark rules, not something domain-general — so this needs a labelled split per benchmark, and the +0.0736 holds only where it has one. That is a materially weaker claim than the headline alone suggests, and it is the first thing a reviewer should be told.

Provenance

What was audited before this deck

Clean

380,677 question-text comparisons across 100 dumps and 1,366,424 judged-slot comparisons: zero mismatches, zero orphans. All 32 train×eval image-overlap pairs clean bar one known case. By-image splits disjoint on all 8. 175 of 182 checkable claims in the written record verified exact.

Fixed

A live currency bug producing penalties up to 12.4× wrong. A cluster bootstrap that had collapsed to i.i.d. while reading as clustered. A contamination check that globbed *.jpg against a directory of *.png and hashed an empty set. A judge file that covered 79.7% of a benchmark and looked finished.

Pin the thread count

The fit is deterministic — five runs on identical inputs agree bitwise, and the pre-refactor code agrees with the current code bitwise too. What moves it is the thread count: across 1/2/4/8 threads the macro spans 0.0033 on one arm. All four arms inside a single run share that count, so the ladder above is internally fair; comparing arms across runs at different counts is not. Consequence for the shipped choice: pooled + ensemble beats pooled + ensemble + self-consistency by +0.0016, and on five of six runs the self-consistency variant is the better arm. Treat those two as a tie and pick on cost, not on the third decimal. Every artifact now records its own argv, thread count and git sha.

Restated since

PathVQA was reported on 1,500 of 3,357 questions and the omitted half is harder. Now backfilled, re-extracted and restated everywhere: the headline moves +0.0802 → +0.0736. I had predicted −0.0022 from that benchmark’s row alone; the real cost is −0.0065, three times larger, because the restored questions also enter TRAINING (108,126 → 112,770 rows) and move every other benchmark slightly. Every figure in this deck is the restated one.

Measured, not assumed

The one real train/eval image overlap — 19 MedPix images — moves the affected delta by −0.00014. Those questions score 0.0000 for both arms, so it could not have inflated anything. It was an estimate in a code comment; it is now an artifact.

Standing rule. Every figure in this deck comes from a named artifact under results/cascade_methods/artifacts/. Judge currency throughout. Bootstrap intervals resample images or benchmarks, never questions i.i.d. — the mistake that produced one retracted “win” earlier in this project.
