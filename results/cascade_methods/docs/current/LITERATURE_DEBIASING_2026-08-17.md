## 0. BOTTOM LINE — the rediscovery verdict, first, because it is the most important thing here

**Three of the four things this round is built on are already in the literature as methods. One is not.
And one of our planned attacks is measurably weaker than it looks — I checked, at zero GPU cost.**

| our claim | status in the field | what survives as ours |
|---|---|---|
| "Estimate the answer prior from a content-free / image-free input and divide it out of a VLM's MCQ scores" (**Attack 1**) | ⛔ **PRIOR ART, 2021 for LLMs and 2024 for MLLMs.** Contextual Calibration (arXiv:2102.09690) `[V*]`; **Post-Hoc Debias for MLLMs — affine calibration with `W = diag(p'_θ)^-1` estimated from image-free inputs — arXiv:2403.05262** `[V*]`. Superseded by Batch Calibration, ICLR 2024 `[V]`. | **Nothing, as a method.** Only the medical-VLM application, the Lingshu instantiation, and (if it fails) the negative result. |
| "MCQ answer-position / option-ID bias exists and can be corrected at inference" | ⛔ **PRIOR ART.** PriDe, ICLR 2024 (arXiv:2309.03882) `[V]`; cyclic/full permutation as its baselines; MMBench **CircularEval** is the VLM benchmarking standard `[S]`; **BOLD** for video-VLMs, ACL 2025 long 162 `[V]`. | The **calibration-residual framing** (§3.3) — precision varies **3.97×** more than confidence across predicted letters. I found no prior art for this. |
| "VLMs over-answer *yes* on binary questions" | ⛔ **PRIOR ART and heavily worked.** POPE (arXiv:2305.10355) `[S]`; **arXiv:2601.12430, whose entire subject is the yes-bias** `[V]`. | **The harness-induced framing.** 2601.12430 explicitly **does not test prompt removal as a baseline** and **includes no medical VQA** `[V*]`. See §4.3 — this is the one genuinely open slot. |
| "Self-consistency helps on MCQ, loses to a trained scorer on free text" | ✅ **MATCHES THE FIELD.** arXiv:2607.09438 `[V*]`. Not novel, but not contradicted — cite it as convergent. | Nothing. Cite and move on. |

### ⚠️ The single most consequential thing I learned, and it is a warning about Attack 1

I measured the live PMC-VQA cell's predicted-letter distribution directly from the existing dump
(§1.2, `[MINE]`). The A-bias the project expects **is really there** — the 7B predicts A on **19.96%** of
items against a **13.23%** gold rate, a 1.51× over-prediction. But:

> **When Lingshu-7B answers "A" on PMC-VQA, the gold answer is very nearly uniform:
> A 0.2869 / B 0.3065 / C 0.2945 / D 0.1121.** `[MINE]`

The A-predictions are **near-uninformative**, not systematically mis-signed. A label-level prior shift
therefore has almost nothing to harvest: blanket-rerouting every `pred=A` to C — the majority gold letter —
moves the cell by **+0.0015**, i.e. **+0.00019 macro.** `[MINE]`

**Consequence for Attack 1.** A prior correction can only pay if it reranks *within each item's own option
log-probs*, not at the label level. And **the live PMC-VQA dump does not contain the option log-prob
vector** — `MedEvalKit/eval_results_lingshu7b_full/{}/PMC_VQA/results.json` carries only `conf` (top-1
probability) and `margin` (top1−top2), not per-letter scores (§1.3, `[MINE]`). So Attack 1 on the live cell
is **not** a free CPU re-analysis; it needs a re-scoring pass over 33,430 items. Budget it as such, and
budget it knowing the label-level headroom is +0.0002 macro.

*(The older `ckpts/gate_lingshu7b_mcq/ckpt_PMC-VQA_nothink_norag.jsonl` **does** carry `opt_logprobs` — but
it is n=500 with gold B+C = 0.6100 and acc 0.6040, i.e. the **wrong track**: the live cell is n=33,430,
B+C = 0.7365, acc 0.5427. Do not develop the method on that dump and port it. `[MINE]`)*

### 1.3 Feasibility check — what is and is not recoverable without GPU

Live PMC-VQA record schema is
`{prompt, answer, choices, response, margin, conf, cum_logprob, gen_toks, latency_s, correct}` `[MINE]`.
**No per-option log-prob vector.** Any contextual-calibration / Batch-Calibration / PriDe test on the live
cell requires one additional forward pass per item (prefill-dominated; `gen_toks = 3`), i.e. **≈1× the cost
of the original greedy pass on that cell**. Wall-clock: **not measured** — the dump's `latency_s` is a
per-item figure under vLLM batching and must not be summed.

From `closed_as_open_2026-08-16.json` `[REPO]`, predicted-yes-rate minus gold-yes base rate, greedy:

**The mechanism replicates on 3 of 3 cells and always in the same direction: the instruction costs

The instruction is at **`MedEvalKit/utils/question_formats.py:29` and `:31`** `[MINE]`, verbatim:

```
prompt = question + "\n" + 'Please output "yes" or "no" and put the answer in one "\\boxed{}".'
prompt = question + "\n" + "Please output 'yes' or 'no'(no extra output)."
```

⚠️ Note line 29 pairs the yes/no instruction with the `\boxed{}` request — and retrospective §5.1 already
established that **asking for `\boxed{}` is itself a reasoning trigger** `[REPO]`. The two are confounded in
the deployed prompt. Whichever half is doing the work, **the fix belongs in our code as an override; do not

## 2. AREA 1 — Contextual calibration and its descendants

### 2.1 The origin, and the fact that it is 5 years old

**Zhao, Wallace, Feng, Klein & Singh. *Calibrate Before Use: Improving Few-Shot Performance of Language
Models.* ICML 2021, arXiv:2102.09690.** `[V*]` Content-free input (`"N/A"`), fit an affine transform that
makes the model's prediction on it uniform, apply to real inputs. Reported **up to 30.0% absolute** gain on
GPT-3/GPT-2 across prompt choices, and — the part that matters more for us — it *stabilises* accuracy across
prompt formats.

### 2.2 ⛔ THE DIRECT PRIOR ART FOR ATTACK 1 — it is already done for multimodal LLMs

**arXiv:2403.05262, *Debiasing Multimodal Large Language Models*.** `[V]` for the abstract, `[V*]` for the
numbers. Two training-free strategies:
- **Post-Hoc Debias** — *"an affine calibration step to adjust the output distribution"* for classification
  and multiple-choice, *"ensuring uniform answer scores when the image is absent"*, with
  **`W = diag(p'_θ)^-1`** estimated by feeding a meaningless vision input (`None` / `Noise` / `Unk`).
- **Visual Debias Decoding** — for open generation, contrasts token log-probs under the correct image
  versus a meaningless one.

Models: LLaVA-1.5 7B/13B, InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL / Qwen-VL-Chat, LLaVA-SFT, LLaVA-RLHF. Benchmarks: POPE,
MME, MMMU, LLaVA-Bench. `[V*]`
Reported `[V*]` — **re-verify against the PDF before citing**: MME LLaVA-v1.5-7B **891.19 → 952.50**
(Post-Hoc Debias/None); 13B **928.81 → 941.19**; POPE-MSCOCO adversarial F1 **≈77.6–79.5 → 82.2–82.27**.

> **This is Attack 1, published in 2024, on a superset of the mechanism, including the image-free prior
> estimate.** Attack 1 cannot be positioned as a new method. It can be positioned as: *the first evaluation
> of MLLM post-hoc debiasing on medical VQA at scale*, and — given §0's measurement — quite possibly as a
> **negative result**, which is this project's established contribution genre.

### 2.3 The method that supersedes it, and that we should use instead

**Zhou, Wan, Huang et al. *Batch Calibration: Rethinking Calibration for In-Context Learning and Prompt
Engineering.* ICLR 2024, arXiv:2309.17249.** `[V]` verbatim: *"BC is zero-shot, inference-only, and incurs
negligible additional costs."* It estimates the contextual bias by **marginalising the model's scores over
the batch of test inputs** — so it needs **no content-free input and no extra forward pass at all**, only
the score vectors you already computed.

Reported `[V*]` (Google Research blog): **+8% / +6% over ICL on PaLM 2-S / PaLM 2-L**; **+6% over
prototypical calibration** on PaLM 2-S; **+3% over contextual calibration** on PaLM 2-L; also validated on
**CLIP** image classification.

Their stated failure case for contextual calibration is directly relevant to us `[V*]`: content-free inputs
are *"not always optimal and may even introduce additional bias, depending on the task type."*

### 2.4 🚨 THE TECHNICAL TRAP THAT WILL SINK A NAIVE IMPLEMENTATION

**Every method in this family calibrates the prediction distribution toward UNIFORM. The gold label prior on
`test_2.csv` is NOT uniform — it is 13.2 / 35.8 / 37.8 / 13.1.** `[REPO]`

A naive BC/CC implementation on PMC-VQA will therefore push predictions **away** from B and C, which are
**73.65% of the gold mass**, and it should be expected to **lose**. The correct target is the *known class
prior*, not uniform.

And that creates a leakage hazard the project has been bitten by before: **estimating the target prior from
the evaluation set's own gold labels is label leakage.** It must come from the PMC-VQA **train** split
(available at `/data/dan/dataset/pmc_vqa_train`, and its B+C ≈ 73.5% is the profile `test_2.csv` matches
`[REPO]`) or be **cross-fit**. A per-cell pick-the-best rule has already earned **+0.0109 macro from
shuffled labels** in this project — run the permutation null.

## 3. AREA 2 — Answer-position / option-order bias

### 3.1 The canonical inference-time correction — take it as given

**Zheng, Zhou, Meng, Zhou & Huang. *Large Language Models Are Not Robust Multiple Choice Selectors.*
ICLR 2024, arXiv:2309.03882.** `[V]` verbatim: selection bias *"primarily stems from LLMs' token bias, where
the model a priori assigns more probabilistic mass to specific option ID tokens (e.g., A/B/C/D)."*
**PriDe** *"first estimates the prior by permutating option contents on a small number of test samples, and
then applies the estimated prior to debias the remaining samples"* — label-free, inference-time.
The abstract gives **no effect sizes**; the paper compares against **Cyclic Permutation** and **Full
Permutation** and claims superiority *"especially in the low-cost scenario"* `[S]`.

**This is the exact shape of the correct Attack-2 method, published two years ago.** The one structural
difference worth noting: PriDe estimates a **global** prior from a small permuted subsample; this project's
already-exhausted "permutation TTA on MCQ" averaged **per item**. A global, low-variance prior is a
different estimator from a per-item average and is not refuted by that negative.

### 3.2 The rest of the family, for completeness

- **MMBench CircularEval** (arXiv:2307.06281) `[S]` — the VLM-benchmarking standard: N cyclic permutations,
  all must be correct. Cost is *less* than N× because a failed pass short-circuits.
- **BOLD** — Loginova, Bezrukov, Shekhar & Kravets, *Addressing Blind Guessing: Calibration of Selection
  Bias in MCQA by Video Language Models*, ACL 2025 long 162 / arXiv:2410.14248. `[V]` A **post-processing
  calibration** for selection bias in video-VLMs; *"the first focused investigation of selection bias in
  video-to-text LLM-powered models."* **No effect sizes in the abstract.** Note the framing they own:
  *"suppressing blind guessing."*
- **arXiv:2509.16805** (Atabuzzaman, Asgarov & Thomas) `[V*]`, already in `LITERATURE_UPDATE_2026-08-11.md` —
  LVLM MCQA selection bias **intensifies with difficulty**; **no medical benchmarks**.
- Medical, text-only: a SIIM 2024 abstract (Gupta, *Answer Positioning Biases in LLM Responses to Medical…*)
  forcibly re-positions MedQA options `[S]`; *Reassessing High-Performing LLMs on Polish Medical Exams*
  (arXiv:2606.12250) reports drops of **28.4 and 31 percentage points** under harder de-biased setups `[S]`.
  Both `[S]` — **verify before citing.**

### 3.3 ✅ The one open slot on this axis

The prior sweep already established (`LITERATURE_UPDATE_2026-08-11.md` §5.4, §0.6) that **essentially no
paper states which PMC-VQA split it used**, and that MedEvalKit hard-codes the one with the *training*
split's answer-position profile. Nothing I found this sweep contradicts that.

What is **additionally** unclaimed, and is the more interesting half:

> **Selection bias is universally framed as a bias in the *prediction distribution*. Nobody I found frames
> it as a defect in the model's *confidence calibration conditional on the predicted label*.** Our
> measurement — precision range 0.400 vs confidence range 0.101 across predicted letters, a **3.97× gap**,
> n = 33,430 `[MINE]` — says the answer letter is a large, free, *residual* signal that any confidence-based
> gate on this cell is throwing away. That is a statement about **routing**, which is this project's actual
> subject, and it is not a rediscovery.

**This is the strongest contribution candidate on the MCQ side, and it is cheaper to establish than a
debiasing method** — it is a property of dumps that already exist for both 7B and 32B.

## 4. AREA 3 — Yes/no bias in binary VQA

### 4.1 Known, named, and heavily worked

- **POPE** (Li et al., arXiv:2305.10355) `[S]` — the canonical source. Balanced 50/50 yes/no probes;
  finds *"models' answers are highly skewed towards being positive."* The **"yes ratio"** is a standard
  reported metric. **Our `predicted_yes_rate` is the same statistic under a different name — use theirs.**
- **arXiv:2601.12430, *System-Mediated Attention Imbalances Make Vision-Language Models Say Yes*.** `[V]`
  An entire paper on the yes-bias. Verbatim: *"the yes-bias, a common form of hallucination in which VLMs
  indiscriminately respond 'yes'"*; attributes it to *"functionally redundant system weights that reduce
  attention to image and textual inputs"*; *"causally redistributing attention from the system modality to
  image and textual inputs substantially suppresses this bias."* Training-free.
  Reported `[V*]` (LLaVA-1.5 7B): HallusionBench simple accuracy **45.64% → 53.31%**, yes-rate
  **89.80% → 59.62%** (target 42.17%); Winoground paired accuracy **7.38% → 19.50%**; SugarCrepe paired
  **15.16% → 25.93%**. Models: LLaVA-1.5 7B/13B, LLaVA-NeXT-Vicuna 7B, Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct. Benchmarks:
  BEAF, HallusionBench, MME, NaturalBench, SugarCrepe, Winoground.
- Also in-family: VaPR (arXiv:2510.01700) `[S]` reports the bias reverses after preference alignment;
  the VCD / CICD / PAI / SCICON decoding family (§6.2).

### 4.2 The load-bearing dataset fact — verify it, then use it

**PathVQA's yes/no subset is balanced by construction: 8,145 "yes" vs 8,189 "no"** across the full 16,334
yes/no instances (arXiv:2003.10286) `[S]` — **snippet only, confirm on the paper before citing.**

If it holds, it is exactly the sentence the PathVQA result needs: **the +0.069 yes-skew cannot be a dataset
prior the model learned, because the dataset has no such prior.** ⚠️ But note the mismatch: **our 3,362-item
closed cell has gold yes rate 0.5402, not 0.500** `[REPO]`. The harness's subset is *not* the balanced full
set. Say 0.5402, cite 8,145/8,189 as the design intent, and do not conflate them.

### 4.3 ✅ Where the contribution actually is — and it is narrow, so state it narrowly

**Not novel:** that VLMs say yes too often. **Not novel:** that this can be corrected training-free.

**Apparently novel, and defensible:**
1. **The bias is induced by the evaluation harness's own instruction string**, and deleting that string
   removes ~97% of it on the largest cell (**+0.0693 → +0.0018**) `[REPO]`, replicating in direction on
   3 of 3 cells (§1.4). arXiv:2601.12430 **does not test prompt removal or rewriting as a baseline** `[V*]`
   — it compares only against PAI, Image×2.0 and AD-HH, all attention-level interventions.
2. **In a medical benchmark.** 2601.12430 covers **no medical VQA** `[V*]`. POPE, HallusionBench,
   NaturalBench, SugarCrepe, Winoground, BEAF, MME — none are medical.
3. **Both currencies agree to 0.0003** (+0.0419 judge / +0.0416 EM) `[REPO]`, which per the project's own
   standing rule is what separates a real effect from a judge artifact.

### 4.4 🚨 The framing hazard — say this out loud before a reviewer does

If the yes-bias is a **harness artifact**, then **+0.0419 is a correction to a measurement, not a gain
available to a deployer** who was never using MedEvalKit's instruction. The honest claim is:

> *"Every PathVQA-closed number produced by MedEvalKit — for every model evaluated with it, including the
> published Lingshu numbers — is depressed by a prompt-induced yes-bias worth ~4 accuracy points on the 7B."*

That is a **benchmarking / evaluation-protocol** claim. It is a good one, and it is in the same genre as the
project's PMC-VQA split finding and MedGemma's public statement that PathVQA has data-quality problems
`[REPO]`. But it is **not** a test-time-compute result, and it must not be summed into a macro gain that is
presented as a method contribution without that caveat attached. **Flagging this now rather than at review
time is the single most useful thing in this section.**

## 5. AREA 4 — Self-consistency / majority vote for VLMs

**Our picture matches the field. Nothing to claim, nothing to fear.**

**arXiv:2607.09438, *Test-Time Scaling for Small VLMs on Multilingual Visual MCQ* (ImageCLEF 2026).** `[V*]`
On EXAMS-V (visual MCQ): **PRM-guided beam search trails plain self-consistency by 0.39 pp at over 8× the
cost**, and *"neither a training-free generative critic nor a trained multimodal PRM beats majority vote
across both policies."* Their conclusion, verbatim `[V*]`: *"the engineering substrate (parser format,
decoding budget) and the policy choice dominate any search or selector method."* Best config 84.1%.

Where it fails, per the same paper `[V*]`: *"chains converge on the same wrong answer, leaving no diversity
for the majority vote to correct"* — which is **exactly** this project's measured pool-degeneracy result on
the closed cells (distinct answers per cell: VQA-RAD 5, PathVQA 9, SLAKE 54, vs 3,919 open) `[REPO]`.

Two things follow:
- Our **+0.0132 [+0.0080,+0.0182] on PMC-VQA in both currencies** `[REPO]` is squarely in-family. Cite
  2607.09438 and 2411.15296-adjacent work as convergent; do not claim novelty.
- Their *"parser format dominates"* finding is an **independent external replication** of this project's
  grader-defect concern (`MedEvalKit/utils/utils.py:112`). It is worth a sentence in the paper because it
  converts a repo-internal caveat into a field-recognised one.

## 6. AREA 5 — Other cheap interventions for a fixed VLM

### 6.1 Prompt-wrapper variance and parseability — cheap, and it touches a known repo defect

**arXiv:2607.09665, *Format Sensitivity Index*.** `[V]` verbatim: *"Prompt wrappers often differ only in
formatting, yet they can change model scores enough to flip leaderboard conclusions."* 140,000 generations,
7 QA tasks, 5 wrapper families, 4 models 7B–72B. **"mean FSI varies by over 30× across models and is largely
explained by compliance failures"**, and *"parseability remains a strong predictor of accuracy even after
controlling for task, model, and wrapper."*

**Why this matters here specifically:** `MedEvalKit/utils/utils.py:112` reduces a bare `"C:"` to the empty
string and falls through to fuzzy matching against option **text** `[REPO]`. That is a **parseability**
failure in exactly this paper's sense. FSI/PSI give us an off-the-shelf, citable instrument for measuring
how much of each cell's accuracy is compliance rather than competence.

### 6.2 Contrastive decoding — the generative sibling of contextual calibration

- **SCICON** — *When Choices Become Priors: Contrastive Decoding for Scientific Figure Multiple-Choice QA*,
  arXiv:2603.28026 (Roh, Jo, Jang & Kang). `[V*]` Training-free; **subtracts text-only option scores from
  image-conditioned scores** for each candidate, targeting *"choice-induced priors encoded within candidate
  text"*, explicitly distinguishing itself from methods using distorted images or perturbed instructions.
  3 backbones, 3 scientific-figure QA datasets including **biomedical images**. *"Consistent accuracy
  improvements"* — **no numbers in the abstract.**
  ⚠️ **For a single-token MCQ answer, SCICON and Post-Hoc Debias are the same computation.** If Attack 1 is
  run, this is the citation for the MCQ case and it is closer to us than 2403.05262 (biomedical images).
- **VCD** (Leng et al. 2024), **CICD** (arXiv:2505.10634), **PAI**, **VAR** `[S]` — the LVLM language-prior
  decoding family. All ~2× decode cost.
- **Classifier-free guidance** at inference (arXiv:2306.17806) `[S]`; *Why are Visually-Grounded Language
  Models Bad at Image Classification?* (arXiv:2405.18415) `[S]` reports LLaVA-1.5-7B on ImageNet
  **22.7% → 35.3%** with probabilistic inference and **→ 47.6%** with CFG. Large, but on image
  classification, and the paper itself notes the approach is *"computationally expensive in practice."*

### 6.3 The image-free forward pass as a *diagnostic* — recommended by a medical paper

**arXiv:2603.20985, *Consistent but Dangerous: Per-Sample Safety Classification Reveals False Reliability in
Medical Vision-Language Models*.** `[V]` verbatim: they *"recommend that deployment evaluations always pair
consistency checks with a text-only baseline: **a single additional forward pass that exposes the false
reliability trap**."* Five medical VLM configs, MIMIC-CXR + PadChest. **LLaVA-Rad Base: 1.5% flip rate while
98.5% of samples are "Dangerous"** (consistent but not image-reliant), with accuracy up to 99.6% and low
entropy — *"invisible to standard confidence-based screening."* r = −0.89 (n=10) between flip rate and
Dangerous fraction.

This is a **free by-product of Attack 1**: the image-free prior pass *is* the text-only baseline. And the
field already has numbers to compare against — on VQA-RAD and SLAKE, text-only reaches **11–33% open-ended
and well above random on MC** `[S]`, versus near-random on a cleaner benchmark.

### 6.4 Medical-VLM calibration — relevant to the gate, not to accuracy

*Just how sure are you? Improving Verbalized Uncertainty Calibration in Medical VQA* (arXiv:2606.27023)
`[S]` — **ECE reduced ≥60%, discrimination improved ≥26%** on three MedVQA benchmarks, MedGemma-4B-IT and
Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct, accuracy preserved. Also *Calibration-Aware Prompt Learning for Medical VLMs*
(arXiv:2509.15226) `[S]`.
⚠️ Temper this hard: the repo already establishes that **monotone post-hoc calibration cannot change AUROC**
`[REPO]`, so "better ECE" is not "better routing". Accuracy-preserving by construction.

### 6.5 Explicitly OUT of scope — do not propose these

Training-time medical debiasing: **DeCoCT** (MICCAI 2025), **CIMB-MVQA**, **Med-BiasX**, **PA-GRPO**
(ACL 2026), counterfactual/causal-intervention Med-VQA `[S]`. All require training; the objective is cheap
test-time interventions. **BendVLM** (arXiv:2411.04420) `[S]` is CLIP-embedding social-bias debiasing —
wrong bias, wrong architecture. **Ask4VG** (arXiv:2606.01044) `[V]` reranks question *rewrites* by
counterfactual risk; VQA-RAD exact accuracy **0.337 → 0.356** with Qwen2-VL-2B — but it trains a risk
estimator and it rewrites the question, which changes the benchmark.

## 7. RANKED SHORTLIST

Costs are in **extra forward passes per item**; FLOP-eq conversions are **not measured** for these and must
not be invented.

### #1 — The answer-letter calibration residual as a routing signal. **ZERO GPU.**
- **Mechanism.** Not a debiaser. Use the predicted letter as a *feature alongside confidence* in the
  gate/veto. Measured: precision range **0.400** vs confidence range **0.101** across letters, 3.97× `[MINE]`.
- **Effect size.** Ours to measure. Directly relevant to the one CI-clean win over always-32B-direct
  (`armcombine_mcqonly_2026-08-11.json`), which the repo already flags as **owing an answer-letter-bias
  audit** `[REPO]`. **This attack discharges that debt.**
- **Cost.** Zero. Both 7B and 32B dumps exist with `conf`, `margin`, `response`, `answer`.
- **Prior negative it resembles.** ~16 recoverability mechanisms at ~0.5–0.6 AUROC.
- **Why it might survive anyway.** Every one of those 16 was a *confidence-derived* signal. This is measured
  to carry **~4× more spread in precision than confidence does**. It is also the only item with no prior
  art (§3.3).
- ⚠️ **Cross-fit and run the permutation null.** Per-cell selection has earned +0.0109 macro from shuffled
  labels here.

### #2 — Extend the yes-bias result from a win into a mechanism, then reframe it. **ZERO GPU.**
- **Mechanism.** Report the yes-rate result as a **3-of-3-cell replication** (§1.4) using POPE's "yes ratio"
  vocabulary, with the sensitivity/specificity decomposition. Separately disentangle the `yes/no` instruction
  from the confounded `\boxed{}` request at line 29.
- **Effect size.** Already measured: +0.0419 judge / +0.0416 EM, both currencies agreeing to 0.0003 `[REPO]`.
- **Cost.** Zero for the reframing. One extra greedy pass per closed cell to de-confound `\boxed{}`.
- **Prior negative it resembles.** None — it is the round's positive.
- **Why it might survive.** It already did. The risk is **framing**, not measurement (§4.4).
- ⚠️ Confirm the 8,145/8,189 PathVQA balance claim on the paper — it is `[S]`, and it is load-bearing.

### #3 — Batch Calibration (**not** contextual calibration) on the MCQ cells, calibrated to the **train-split
prior**, not to uniform.
- **Mechanism.** Marginalise option scores over the test batch (arXiv:2309.17249 `[V]`); target the PMC-VQA
  *train* prior (`/data/dan/dataset/pmc_vqa_train`), never the eval gold prior.
- **Effect size elsewhere.** +8%/+6% over ICL on PaLM 2-S/L; +3% over CC `[V*]`.
- **Cost.** **One re-scoring pass over 33,430 items** to obtain the option log-prob vectors the live dump
  lacks (§1.3) — prefill-dominated, ≈1× the original greedy pass. BC itself is then free.
- **Prior negative it resembles.** Permutation/multi-view TTA on MCQ (dead); all training-free selectors at
  the random-pick floor.
- **Why it might survive.** BC needs no permutations and no content-free input, so it is not the estimator
  that already failed. **But temper expectations hard:** §0 measured the label-level headroom at
  **+0.0002 macro**, and §2.4 shows the naive uniform-target version should be expected to *lose*.
- ⚠️ **This is prior art as a method (§2.2). Run it to get the number, not to claim the mechanism.**

### #4 — Prompt-wrapper / parseability audit (FSI, PSI) across all 8 cells.
- **Mechanism.** K wrapper variants per cell; report the accuracy range (FSI) and parse-rate range (PSI).
- **Effect size elsewhere.** FSI varies >30× across models, *"largely explained by compliance failures"*;
  parseability predicts accuracy after controlling for task/model/wrapper `[V]`.
- **Cost.** K extra greedy passes per cell. Image prefill is shared under prefix caching, so K is cheap
  relative to K× naive — the repo measured 8 samples at 2.28 FLOP-eq with caching on `[REPO]`.
- **Prior negative it resembles.** Resolution sweeps; the "±0.008 open-text reproducibility" caveat.
- **Why it might survive.** It is not trying to win — it is **quantifying the size of the class of defect
  this whole round is built on**, across all 8 cells, with an external instrument and a citation. It
  converts "we found two bias bugs" into "we measured the format-sensitivity envelope of the suite."
- ⚠️ Needs a MATCHED in-session control arm per the ±0.008 rule.

### #5 — SCICON-style option-prior contrastive decoding on the MCQ cells.
- **Mechanism.** Score options with the image, score them text-only, subtract (arXiv:2603.28026 `[V*]`).
- **Effect size.** *"Consistent improvements"*, **no published numbers** — a real weakness of the citation.
- **Cost.** For a single-letter answer: **one extra text-only prefill per item**, and it is the same pass
  #3 needs. **Run #3 and #5 from one re-scoring job.**
- **Prior negative it resembles.** Same family as #3.
- **Why it might survive.** It is item-conditional (it subtracts *this item's* option-text prior), which is
  exactly the level §0 showed is required — the label-level shift is worth +0.0002, so only an item-level
  correction can pay.

### #6 — Image-free / text-only baseline pass as a published diagnostic.
- **Mechanism.** Answer every item with the image removed; report the blind accuracy per cell.
- **Effect size.** Not an accuracy method. Reference points: text-only 11–33% open-ended and *"well above
  random"* on MC for VQA-RAD/SLAKE `[S]`; 98.5% "Dangerous" for LLaVA-Rad on PadChest `[V]`.
- **Cost.** Free if #3 or #5 runs — it *is* their prior-estimation pass.
- **Why it matters.** A medical-VLM paper explicitly recommends it as *"a single additional forward pass"*
  `[V]`, and it feeds the project's existing contamination/benchmark-quality thread. Also the cleanest
  possible check that the PathVQA yes-bias fix is not just shifting a blind prior.

### #7 — System-attention redistribution (arXiv:2601.12430). **Do not run.**
- Training-free with large reported effects (yes-rate 89.80% → 59.62% `[V*]`), but: requires attention
  hooks in HF transformers, is tuned on LLaVA, and their baseline yes-rate (89.8%) is far above ours
  (60.9%). **A free prompt edit already removes 97% of our yes-bias.** Dominated. Cite as the *mechanistic
  explanation* for why the instruction does what it does; do not implement.
