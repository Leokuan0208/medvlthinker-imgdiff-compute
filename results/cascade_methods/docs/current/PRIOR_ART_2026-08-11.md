## 3. PER-PAPER ANALYSIS

### 3.1 P1 — Wasserstein Equilibrium Decoding (arXiv:2605.18313) — **the nearest neighbour to our open-text arm**

**What it claims.** Extends game-theoretic (Bayesian Decoding Game, BDG) decoding from text-only
closed-ended NLP to vision-language open-ended medical VQA, with a semantically-aware Wasserstein-1
stopping criterion replacing lexical rank-matching. Claims consistent, statistically significant gains
over greedy and discriminative baselines. `[V-abs]`

**What it MEASURES.** `[V-html]`
- **Models:** Qwen3-VL-2B / 4B / 8B, Gemma-3-4B, MedGemma-4B. Five decoding strategies × **5 seeds**.
- **Datasets:** VQA-RAD and PathVQA, **open-ended subsets only, yes/no questions excluded**.
- **n:** ⚠️ **the paper does not state per-dataset open-subset test n.** It reports dataset totals
  (VQA-RAD ~315 images / 3,515 QA pairs; PathVQA 4,998 images / 32,799 QA pairs), which are *not* the
  evaluation n. **`[UNVERIFIED]` — do not report an n for this paper.**
- **Metric:** exact match, token F1, SapBERT cosine, and the headline **"Judge Accuracy" via Grok 4.1 Fast
  as VLM-judge**.
- **Pool:** temperature-diverse nucleus sampling at T ∈ {0.5, 1.0}, up to **8 unique candidates** after
  canonicalization (Nmax = 16 calls).
- **Headline numbers:** VQA-RAD Qwen3-VL-2B greedy **33.50±0.00** → BDG-W **37.04±0.94** (p<0.01);
  Qwen3-VL-4B greedy **36.50** → **40.30**; Qwen3-VL-8B greedy **40.50** → **39.29** (a loss);
  Gemma-3-4B **40.00** → **42.30**; MedGemma-4B **54.00** → **56.70**. PathVQA Qwen3-VL-2B **4.98** →
  **7.17**; Gemma-3-4B **18.91** → **23.89**.
- **Scaling:** "The improvement is inversely correlated with parameter count: BDG-W yields a +3.5/+2.2 pp
  gain … for 2B parameters but only −1.2/+1.2 pp for 8B."

**⚠️ THE SWEEP MISCHARACTERISES THIS PAPER'S MECHANISM.** `LITERATURE_UPDATE_2026-08-11.md:101` describes
it as "generator–verifier **best-of-N** for medical VQA on small VLMs". It is **not best-of-N with a
verifier**. It is a **training-free decode-time equilibrium**: generator and "verifier" are both derived
from the *same base VLM* by prompt-conditioning (teacher-forced scoring under "correct"/"incorrect"
prompts; the verifier reverses the conditioning), updated by a Markovian no-regret rule until
convergence. **There is no trained verifier and no separate reward model.** `[V-html]`

**What it pre-empts.**
- ✅ **"Test-time compute on a small VLM improves open-ended medical VQA on VQA-RAD and PathVQA."** Taken.
  Published 2026-05-18, on the same two open datasets we use.
- ✅ **"Semantic (not lexical) dedup/consensus over a temperature-sampled candidate pool of 8."** Taken —
  our `norm()`-based canonicalisation of 8 sampled answers is the same design move.
- ✅ **"A small model with test-time compute surpasses a greedy model twice its size"** on VQA-RAD
  (2B+BDG 37.04 > 4B greedy 36.50). Our claim shape, at a **2× ratio**.

**What it does NOT pre-empt.**
- ❌ **A *trained* verifier.** Theirs is zero-shot self-derived. Our LoRA outcome verifier is the only
  thing in this project that broke the training-free floor `[REPO]`, and P3 (§3.3) is external evidence
  that the untrained kind fails. **This is our strongest surviving open-text novelty.**
- ❌ **Cross-model-size escalation.** "Cascades, escalation, routing to larger models: none mentioned."
  `[V-html]` Their method never calls a bigger model.
- ❌ **Cost accounting.** They report convergence *iterations* (BDG-W 27.46±1.52 → 14.02±1.95, ~20% fewer
  than BDG-classic) and explicitly no FLOPs, no ms/sample, no energy. `[V-html]` Our R32=4.57
  as-charged / 3.816 honest FLOP-eq accounting, latency and joules have no counterpart.
- ❌ **MCQ.** Open-ended only, yes/no excluded. Our 5 MCQ cells (39,879 of 42,224 items) are untouched.
- ❌ **Scale.** Their largest model is 8B; our bar is a 32B.
- ❌ **Decontamination.** No train/eval disjointness analysis (their method needs no training, so the
  issue does not arise — which is itself a point in their favour, not ours).

**⛔ PROTOCOL-COMPARABILITY: NOT COMPARABLE. Do not put their numbers in a table with ours.** Four
independent blockers: (i) different judge model (Grok 4.1 Fast vs our judge), (ii) unstated evaluation n,
(iii) different open/closed split boundary (they exclude yes/no; our `PATH_VQA_open` n=1500 and
`VQA_RAD_open` n=200 come from our own split), (iv) different generator families entirely. The
STANDING CAVEAT already forbids comparing our open-text cells across *serving configurations* (±0.008/cell,
larger than our whole +0.0008 delta) — comparing across *judges and splits* is strictly worse.

**Surviving novelty, stated precisely:** *They do training-free equilibrium decoding on 2–8B general VLMs,
open-ended VQA-RAD/PathVQA, unstated n, Grok-judged, no cost accounting and no larger model. We do a
trained-verifier best-of-N inside a 7B→32B escalation cascade over 8 cells / n=42,224 with FLOP, latency
and energy accounting and a decontaminated verifier. The overlap is the pool-of-8 + semantic dedup
substrate; the novelty is the trained verifier, the escalation, and the cost model.*

**What it claims.** Test-time collaboration gains should be decomposed into measurable factors, and
"gains are bounded first by the oracle gap and then by signal fidelity." Offers it as "a practical
pre-deployment diagnostic: estimate the oracle gap, then measure coverage, signal fidelity, and harm
before investing in collaboration." `[V-abs]`

**What it MEASURES.** `[V-html]`
- **Benchmarks:** LiveCodeBench (1,055 tasks; 2,888 task-seed observations), MATH Level-5 hard (250 × 3
  seeds = 750), GPQA-Diamond (198 × 3 seeds = 594), HumanEval+ (164) as a saturation anchor.
- **Model:** Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-BF16 as primary. Smaller models "only in follow-up, robustness, or routing
  analyses."
- **Pool:** k = 5.
- **Their decomposition (additive):** `gain = P(recoverable ∧ defined) × q − P(reference correct ∧ defined) × h`,
  with *recoverable mass* = P(reference wrong ∧ any@k correct), *signal coverage* = P(signal defined),
  *conditional quality* q = P(selected passes | recoverable, defined), *conditional harm* h = P(selected
  fails | reference passes, defined).
- **Headline numbers:** LiveCodeBench first-sample 72.33%; public-test verifier **+8.14 pp
  [6.99, 9.36]**; generated-test **+2.70 pp**; same-family LLM selector **+3.50 pp**; oracle any@5
  **+11.74 pp**. Capture of recoverable cases: **69.32%** (public-test), **23.30%** (generated-test),
  **58.70%** (LLM). Harm: **0.10%** (L4-gen) and **4.69%** (L1). MATH **+4.67 pp** symbolic vs
  **−3.20 pp** for the LLM selector. GPQA oracle gap only **+3.03 pp**, LLM selector **−1.68 pp** net,
  **87.54%** of pools answer-identical.

**What it pre-empts. This one is real — and the sweep's verdict at
`LITERATURE_UPDATE_2026-08-11.md:103` ("Cite, do not claim priority") is CORRECT and I confirm it.**
- ✅ **The decomposition itself**, as a named, published, general framework, dated **2026-07-20**.
- ✅ **The conclusion that coverage binds before selection.** Their "gains are bounded first by the oracle
  gap and then by signal fidelity" is the same claim as our "the coverage wall is ~4.5× the selection
  wall" `[REPO]`. **Our version is first written down in `progress/progress_August_05.md` and
  `docs/current/COMPARATIVE_VERIFIER_2026-08-05.md`, both dated 2026-08-05 — 16 days after their
  posting.** We must cite them for the conclusion. (The *selection wall* alone is older here —
  `progress/progress_July_06.md` — but the coverage-vs-selection *ranking* is not.)
- ✅ **"Harm to correct outputs" as a first-class term.**

**What it does NOT pre-empt.**
- ❌ **Any vision-language or medical evaluation.** "No vision-language, medical, or cross-modal
  benchmarks are included." `[V-html]` Code, math, and text MCQ only.
- ❌ **The exact multiplicative identity.** They have **no** identity of the form `selected = oracle × η`.

**✅ WE CAN AND SHOULD ADOPT THEIR FRAMEWORK — I computed our result in it this round.** Their

### 3.5 ⚠️ CORRECTION 1 — "selection efficiency 0.78–0.81 is a FIELD CONSTANT" DOES NOT SURVIVE DEFINITION-MATCHING

`LITERATURE_UPDATE_2026-08-11.md` §0.3 and its table at line 304 place agentic boosting's **78.3%** beside
this project's **sel_eff 0.775204 / 0.810627** and conclude selection efficiency ≈ 0.78–0.81 is a field
constant, "not our failure". **These are two different quantities and the agreement is a coincidence.**

**Our `sel_eff` is a ratio of accuracies.** Verified in code this round: `genframe_data.py:518-560`
computes `rec = 1 if 1 in sl` (i.e. *the pool contains a correct answer* = oracle-correct) and
`sel_eff = mean(got | rec)`. Since `got = 0` whenever `rec = 0`, this is **exactly `selected / oracle`**.

**⛔ AND THE DOCSTRING AT `src/training_methods/genframe_data.py:21` IS WRONG.** It reads:
```
sel_eff  = mean(pick is correct | pool recoverable) = (selected-greedy)/(oracle-greedy)
```
The second equality is **false** — it holds only if greedy = 0. Measured: `selected/oracle = 0.7752`
but `(selected−greedy)/(oracle−greedy) = 0.2029`. (Lines 22–24 of the same docstring *do* warn "the
difference form is an identity only when greedy == random-slot accuracy, so DO NOT recompute it that
way", so the code and its own caveat are right — but the headline line contradicts them, and the sweep
appears to have read the headline line.) **This is a docstring defect in a frozen-metric file and should
be fixed; the metric itself is correct and the null test passes.**

**The three definitions in circulation, all computed on our verified pool** `[MINE, on [REPO] inputs]`:

| definition | formula | ours (incumbent) | ours (deployed) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **M1** ratio of accuracies — *what we publish* | selected / oracle | **0.7752** | **0.8106** |
| **M2** gain conversion vs greedy — *what P5 reports* | (sel−greedy)/(oracle−greedy) | **0.2029** | **0.3285** |
| **M3** gain conversion vs random slot pick | (sel−rand)/(oracle−rand) | **0.3056** | **0.4150** |

(random-slot pick, closed form, `genframe_data.py::random_pick`: acc **0.423348**, sel_eff **0.676260**.)

**The matched comparison, both directions** `[MINE]`:

| | M1 (accuracy ratio) | M2 (gain conversion) |

| **P5 agentic boosting** (67.0 → 76.4, oracle 79.0) | **0.9671** | **0.7833** ← the number the sweep quotes |

**⇒ Under M1 we are 19.2 points below P5. Under M2 we are 58.0 points below P5 (45.5 below, deployed).

The correct, defensible, and more interesting statement is:

> *Reported selection efficiencies are not comparable across papers unless the denominator is stated. On

> explained by their own thesis: reliable amplification requires a local soundness signal (execution,
> proof checking), and medical open-text VQA has none. Hu's decomposition localises this further — our

### 3.6 ⚠️ CORRECTION 2 — P6 IS QUOTED SELECTIVELY, AND ITS OTHER HALF IS THE STRONGEST THREAT TO OUR FRAMING

`LITERATURE_UPDATE_2026-08-11.md` §0.1 cites P6 (Sammani et al., arXiv:2606.28864) as one of three pillars

**But it is finding (2) of three, and finding (1) — the abstract's own headline — points the other way**
`[V-abs]`:

> "1) different from previous findings, **small, well-performing models benefit the most from test-time
> scaling, enabling performance improvements of up to around 30%, reaching large models performance, and
> often outperforming them**"

and in the body `[V-html]`:

> "small models with test-time scaling can outperform larger ones. For example, **Qwen3-VL-4B with simple
> CoT and S-CoT already surpasses the baseline performance of Qwen3-VL-32B** on HallusionBench, WeMath and

with **+13% on LogicVista for Qwen3-VL-4B+CoT over the Qwen3-VL-32B baseline — an 8× parameter ratio,
larger than our 4.57×**.

**Why this is the single most dangerous item in this document.** Our entire deliverable is "7B + test-time
compute ties, but does not beat, always-32B-direct." P6 is a same-family 4B-vs-32B *existence proof of the
positive result*, published 2026-06-27, and its comparison shape is identical to ours (small model + TTS
vs the large model's direct baseline). **A reviewer who reads only P6's abstract will conclude our
negative is a weaker execution of a solved problem.** Citing only P6's perception half would look like
selective quotation of a paper whose headline is the opposite, and would be caught immediately.

**The honest reconciliation — and it holds.** P6's own two findings partition the space by benchmark type,
and every benchmark where their small model wins is on the *reasoning/hallucination* side:
- Their wins: **HallusionBench, WeMath, LogicVista** — classified reasoning-focused or mixed `[V-html]`.
- Their degradation: **RealWorldQA, A-OKVQA** — perception-focused `[V-html]`.
- **No medical benchmark at all** `[V-html]`.

Our suite sits almost entirely on the losing side of their own partition: seven of our eight cells are
perception (PMC-VQA, SLAKE-closed/open, VQA-RAD-closed/open, PathVQA-closed/open), and **our one
reasoning-heavy cell, MedXpertQA-MM, is at chance for both models — 7B 0.2615 / 32B-direct 0.3065**

suite does not contain: we have no cell that is simultaneously reasoning-heavy *and* above chance for the

attains somewhat non-trivial success rates") in the negative.

**This is a good outcome for the paper, if stated first rather than discovered by a reviewer.** It turns
"we tied" into "we identify, and measure, the boundary of a published positive result: small-model TTS
beats a much larger model on reasoning-type benchmarks and degrades on perception-type ones; medical VQA
is perception-heavy, and its one reasoning-heavy benchmark is at chance for models of this scale, so the
enabling condition is unavailable." **Cite P6 for BOTH findings, prominently, and own the partition.**

## 5. HONEST POSITIONING — what is genuinely novel, ranked by defensibility

Ranking criterion: *would this survive a reviewer who has read all eight papers above?*

**TIER 1 — defensible, no collision found.**

1. **The trained-verifier-inside-a-cross-model-size-cascade for medical open-text VQA, with a
   decontaminated verifier and full cost accounting.** P1 does TTS on medical open VQA but training-free,
   single-model, no cost. P3 shows the untrained kind fails. P2/P4/P5 are not multimodal. Nothing found
   combines trained verifier + escalation + FLOP/latency/energy on medical VQA. **Strongest surviving
   contribution.**
2. **The decontamination result.** A verifier scoring items it was trained on inflated the open-text arm
   enough to flip the headline: −0.0119 [−0.0188, −0.0052] macro, retiring "beats always-32B-direct" to a

   failure mode (reward hacking, not train/eval overlap) in a different domain. **A published, honest,
   self-inflicted retraction with a measured attribution is rare and is a genuine contribution.**
3. **Format-aware MCQ-vs-open routing**, grounded in the measured routing-AUROC gap (~0.6 on 4-option MCQ
   vs ~0.87 on free text) `[REPO]`. No paper found routes on answer format. Bounded search, so
   `[UNVERIFIED]` as a clearance — but P1, P2, P4, P6 all evaluate a single format and none discusses the
   distinction.

**TIER 2 — defensible with an explicit concession.**
