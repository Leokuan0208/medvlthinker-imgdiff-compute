Two things follow. The zero-information router cannot get below 1.000x at all — so unlike a wide-CI “tie”, nothing here is handed over by the tolerance. And routing whole datasets gets only to 0.902x, which is exactly the policy “send PMC-VQA to the 7B and everything else to the 32B”: the between-cell component is one cell.

Is there real within-item skill?

The permutation null shuffles labels inside each cell, so it destroys within-cell signal but keeps each cell's mean advantage. Comparing the router against it at matched cost isolates within-item skill from dataset recognition.

What this does not show

Eval-selected frontier points are upper bounds. Choosing the cheapest of 649 swept policies by whether its bootstrap CI clears −0.0029 is a multiple-comparison procedure, and the feasibility flag is visibly noisy — 0.844x qualifies, 0.857x and 0.870x do not, 0.883x does again. Read the frontier's shape, not its cheapest qualifying point.

The prompt-only router does not survive honest selection. Choosing configuration and threshold inside training folds only lands at 0.731x with 0/10 seeds meeting the constraint: that selector minimises cost against a point-estimate screen, which does not control the CI at the operating point. The hybrid's nested selection does hold, but it picks an arm per cell and therefore needs to know which benchmark a question came from.

It saves compute, not memory. A router that can send a question straight to the 32B still needs the 32B resident, and the 7B as well — 46.95 GiB against the bar's 31.5 GiB.
